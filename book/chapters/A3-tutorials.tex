\chapter{Tutorials}
\label{app:tutorials}

This appendix brings together online books, videos, newsletters, blogs and code repositories
collected in the author's notes and literature folder while writing this book.
Titles and names were checked against YouTube, GitHub and the publishers'
own sites in September 2026, with further readings and link checks on
10, 14 and 22 October and 2 and 6 November 2026. Repositories that have moved are listed at their
current address. Web material is taken down and renamed more often than
papers, so some links will break. If one fails, prefix it with
\url{https://web.archive.org/web/} to reach the Internet Archive's most
recent copy, where one exists.

\emph{The Turing Way}, by The Turing Way Community, is a free online book
covering reproducibility, project design, communication, collaboration and
research ethics. Its guides can be consulted by topic as questions arise in
your work.\\
\url{https://book.the-turing-way.org/}

\emph{Fundamentals of Deep Learning}, from NVIDIA's Deep Learning Institute,
provides practical exercises in Jupyter notebooks using PyTorch. The course
starts with image classification, then introduces convolutional networks,
data augmentation and adapting pretrained models. Its examples let readers
practise training and checking a model before tackling the language-model
architectures in the Stanford course below. Slides accompany the notebooks.\\
\url{https://github.com/NVDLI/fundamentals-of-deep-learning}

\emph{CME 295 -- Transformers \& Large Language Models} is taught by Afshine
Amidi and Shervine Amidi at Stanford University. It covers how language
models are built, trained, evaluated and used as agents. The syllabus links
slides and recorded lectures on YouTube, released week by week during the
autumn 2026 term. It assumes calculus, linear algebra and basic machine
learning, so it goes deeper than this book. The first three lectures provide
a useful route through the foundations: tokens, embeddings and attention;
model architectures and text generation; then pretraining and subsequent
tuning. Read the first alongside \textcite{vaswani2017attention} and
\Cref{ch:whynow}. The third distinguishes learning from example answers from
learning which answers are preferred. The fourth covers reinforcement learning
for language models, from policy gradients to reward design, which is where
reasoning models come from. The fifth covers the engineering that makes
training and serving feasible: flash attention, distributed training and
inference optimisations, the supply side of \Cref{ch:hardware}.\\
\url{https://cme295.stanford.edu/syllabus/}

The course's multilingual cheatsheet provides a compact reference for the
terminology and equations used in the lectures.\\
\url{https://cme295.stanford.edu/cheatsheet/}

\emph{Large Language Models and their applications in Bioinformatics} is a
free webinar series from EMBL-EBI Training: six sessions held between
26 February and 16 April 2025, with the recordings openly available. They
cover transformers and language models, building a dataset for a model,
generated summaries for protein families at InterPro, BioChatter and
LLM-driven bioscience, extracting domain knowledge from scientific text, and
a closing question session. The speakers are Andrew Green, Melanie Vollmar,
Matthias Blum, Sebastian Lobentanzer and Tushar Mandloi; Green and Blum are
among the authors of the curation perspective in \Cref{ch:applications}. Its
examples are molecular, but the InterPro session shows generated summaries
in production with curators in the loop, which is the pattern
\Cref{ch:applications} recommends. The series page is listed by its title
under the events of EMBL-EBI Training.\\
\url{https://www.ebi.ac.uk/training/events/}

The training infrastructure of this book's own readership ages more slowly
than videos and newsletters, and its courses are free. \emph{The
Carpentries} teach foundational coding and data skills to researchers; the
Data Carpentry ecology workshop starts from a species survey table. TeSS,
ELIXIR's training portal, indexes courses and materials from the life-science
research infrastructures of Europe. \emph{EMBL-EBI Training} offers
on-demand courses on the EBI's data resources. GBIF's \emph{Biodiversity
Data Use} course and \emph{The OBIS manual} cover the occurrence-data
infrastructures that \Cref{ch:aireadydata} describes.\\
\url{https://carpentries.org/lessons/}\\
\url{https://datacarpentry.org/ecology-workshop/}\\
\url{https://tess.elixir-europe.org/}\\
\url{https://www.ebi.ac.uk/training/}\\
\url{https://docs.gbif.org/course-data-use/en/}\\
\url{https://manual.obis.org/}

\section{Blogs}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Simon Willison's Weblog}, Simon Willison.\\
    \url{https://simonwillison.net}
  \item \emph{Blog}, Yoshua Bengio.\\
    \url{https://yoshuabengio.org/en/blog}
  \item \emph{Blog}, Library Innovation Lab, Harvard University. Jacqueline
    J. Dearborn's talk
    \href{https://lil.law.harvard.edu/blog/2026/09/24/who-will-steward-humanity-s-public-data/}{``Who Will Steward Humanity's Public Data? Collective Global Stewardship for the Long Horizon''}
    asks whether public data will still be understood, maintained and funded
    in fifty years. She gave it on 10 September 2026; the post of 24 September
    carries the transcript and the recording. Her career began digitising
    collections for the Biodiversity Heritage Library. She argues that an
    infrastructure includes the people who hold its unwritten knowledge, and
    that shared open-source platforms are easier to keep alive than bespoke
    ones. She cites an Invest in Open Infrastructure analysis of grant funding
    in which only about a fifth of the money that reached infrastructure went
    to core operations. Read it with the infrastructure section of
    \Cref{ch:aireadydata}.\\
    \url{https://lil.law.harvard.edu/blog/}
  \item \emph{Fedora Magazine}, the Fedora Project.\\
    \url{https://fedoramagazine.org}
  \item \emph{The official Red Hat blog}, Red Hat.
    Four saved articles provide starting points.
    ``The future of AI demands a hybrid foundation'' (published 3 June 2026)
    discusses placing workloads across local and hosted infrastructure.
    ``Why flexibility is non-negotiable in the Middle East's AI
    transformation journey'' (4 June 2026) discusses data location and staff skills.
    ``Why Red Hat is building secure agent onboarding''
    (28 September 2026) describes enforcing access policy outside the model.
    Ganesh Murthy's
    \href{https://www.redhat.com/en/blog/building-ai-powered-multimodal-compliance-monitor-training-tracking-chat}{``Building an AI-powered multimodal compliance monitor: From training to tracking to chat''}
    (30 September 2026) follows video detections into a database queried through
    a language-model interface. It describes read-only database access through
    MCP and application checks that restrict queries to the selected video.
    This is an implementation example, not evidence of ecological accuracy.
    These are vendor perspectives; \Cref{ch:localfirst,ch:security} discuss
    their practical implications.\\
    \url{https://www.redhat.com/en/blog}
  \item \emph{Blue Continuum}, David C. Krakauer's essay

  \item \emph{Undark Magazine}, Jonathan Jacobs and Patrick Boyle's

    \url{https://github.com/meringlab/string-mcp}
  \item \emph{interpro-mcp}: an MCP server for InterPro protein families and
    domains, used in \Cref{ch:aireadydata}. Judge any MCP server as a program

    \url{https://github.com/ebi-pf-team/interpro-mcp}
  \item \emph{ellmer}: calls language models from R, local or hosted, and

    Posit; on CRAN.\\
    \url{https://github.com/tidyverse/ragnar}
  \item \emph{vitals}: large language model evaluation in R, from Posit; does
    at scale what the scoring script of \Cref{ch:testing} does; on CRAN.\\
    \url{https://github.com/tidyverse/vitals}
  \item \emph{openalexR}: bibliographic records from OpenAlex, from rOpenSci;
    on CRAN. An alternative to the hand-written calls of
    \Cref{ch:weeklyliterature}.\\
    \url{https://github.com/ropensci/openalexR}
  \item \emph{europepmc}: the Europe PMC web service from R, from rOpenSci;
    on CRAN.\\
    \url{https://github.com/ropensci/europepmc}
  \item \emph{rgbif}: the GBIF API from R, from rOpenSci, including backbone
    matching of a checklist of names; on CRAN.\\
    \url{https://github.com/ropensci/rgbif}
  \item \emph{weekly-literature}: the weekly literature review of
    \Cref{ch:weeklyliterature}: the R scripts, the fixture week, the
    assistant skill and the provenance logs of every run.\\
    \url{https://github.com/savvas-paragkamian/weekly-literature}
